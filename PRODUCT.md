# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Self-directed CFA Level I candidates studying independently, often under time pressure and across desktop and mobile devices. They need to understand concepts, practise exam-style questions, diagnose mistakes, retain weak areas, and build confidence without requiring an instructor to interpret the experience for them.

## Product Purpose

CFA Wizard is a self-study application that turns the CFA Level I curriculum into guided learning, exam-style item practice, optional case-based learning, mistake diagnosis, spaced recall, mock exams, analytics, and calculator workflows. Success means candidates can generate trustworthy practice, understand why each answer is correct or incorrect, identify recurring traps, and make measurable progress toward exam readiness.

## Brand Personality

Calm, expert, and rigorous. The product should communicate financial and pedagogical credibility without feeling intimidating, theatrical, or needlessly dense. It should guide candidates with clear language, visible progress, and reassuring feedback at high-stakes moments.

## Anti-references

Avoid the visual and interaction density of a trading terminal, decorative “institutional” jargon, gamification that trivializes exam preparation, and generic AI-generated dashboard patterns. Avoid interfaces that require expert product knowledge before a candidate can begin studying.

## Design Principles

1. Make the next useful study action obvious.
2. Earn trust through accurate content, transparent generation, and explainable answers.
3. Reduce cognitive load without hiding the rigor of the curriculum.
4. Turn mistakes into specific, actionable learning loops.
5. Treat mobile study, keyboard access, and resilient recovery as core workflows.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Support complete keyboard operation, visible focus, sufficient contrast, reduced motion, semantic structure, screen-reader labeling, comfortable touch targets, and responsive layouts suitable for mobile self-study.
